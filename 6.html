As intelligence acts on our behalf, giving our word becomes more consequential.

Signet makes that act personal, physical and verifiable.

The gesture

A pause before you seal.

Review the words, work or request on your phone. Touch the ring to sign. Your signature is bound to exactly what you approved. Change the content, and the signature no longer matches.

Explore the possibilities

One instrument. Applications for your work, your authority and your access.

Each application uses the same act. Touch the ring to a phone that requests a signature, and the ring signs with a key made inside its chip. The key cannot be copied out.

Functions depend on compatible software and integrations. Availability is confirmed with Jim before purchase.

Sign your work

Put your signature to something you created, said or shared.

Two applications

  • Sign a text, image or document

Give your authority

Give your word to instructions, permissions and decisions.

Five applications

Your signature records an instruction. Enforcing it needs a connected system that checks for your signature.

  • Delegate to a person

    Availability to confirm

    Give someone signed instructions they can verify came from you.

    Needs compatible signing and verification software.

  • Set an AI mandate

    Availability to confirm

    Create a signed record of an AI’s task, the authority you give it and the limits you set.

    Needs compatible software. The limits hold only where a connected system checks them.

  • Approve a protected action

    In development

    Require your signature before a connected system carries out a specified action.

    Needs a system integrated with the Request Seal SDK.

  • Manage agent permissions

    Availability to confirm

    Grant, change or revoke what an agent can do for you, such as sending tokens, transferring digital assets, updating your profile or using approved apps.

  • Approve a transaction

    In development

    Use your ring as a signer on a shared wallet. Its signature would count towards the approvals the wallet requires before a transaction proceeds.

The object

For five thousand years, a signet carried a person’s word. In Signet 01 the mark is the machine: the labyrinth is the antenna, tuned to the chip at its centre. No screen. No battery. Nothing to charge.

Designer

Jim Hannon-Tan developed his practice in Melbourne, Milan, London and Amsterdam, working with Alessi, Tom Dixon and Marcel Wanders. He designed Signet 01 in Adelaide.

‘The bridge between a physical object and the digital world is usually an NFC tag added to an existing design. That felt like a missed opportunity. I wondered whether the antenna could become the object itself.

‘In its spiral I recognised a labyrinth, a single path winding towards a centre. Silver is both precious and a conductor, so the form and the material could make the technology work.

‘That led me back to the signet ring, a personal seal carried on the hand. With a cryptographic key held inside, a touch could become a signature.’
